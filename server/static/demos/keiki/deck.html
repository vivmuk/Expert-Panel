Research brief · AI-generated from public pages

Public Keiki pages position the product as customer-facing agents that live in existing messaging channels, with one agent config served across SMS, iMessage, WhatsApp, Slack, Telegram, Teams, and email[1][2][3]. For a first launch, prioritize channels where the product team already has customer identity and consent: start with iMessage and WhatsApp for consumer-facing support/use cases, and Slack for B2B or team-facing workflows, because Keiki's homepage explicitly names iMessage, WhatsApp, and Slack as the headline channels[1]. Hold SMS and email as secondary because they are listed but not positioned as primary[2], and defer Telegram and Teams until the first channel is stable. Before launch, the team needs: a written job description and agent voice/boundaries[4]; product knowledge sources such as docs, website, and app via API or MCP server[1]; one cloud-agent config with instructions, model, and feature toggles, synced with kit.sync()[3]; scoped API credentials issued through the dashboard with the agents:chat scope and the API gateway enabled[7]; channel connection details handled through OAuth rather than chat-shared tokens[5]; and an eval/test plan, since pricing lists eval runs and logs/traces[6]. Assumption: the team has not yet chosen channels or built the config; public pages do not state a recommended launch order. Assumption: phone-line costs of $300/mo each apply if SMS or voice-adjacent numbers are used, per pricing[6]. Keiki is invite-only, so access should be requested before launch planning[5]. This note is based only on public pages; the company did not commission it.

    07 Synthesis

    What the panel converged on, and what it can't see.

    Synthesis

    The experts converge on launching a single, low-blast-radius asynchronous text channel — most plausibly an owned web/help surface — scoped narrowly to public-content and triage questions with a hard human handoff, while holding email, messaging, and voice behind explicit gates. The real debate is sequencing logic: identity-and-consent-first strategists (Mara, Nadia, Corinne, Lena) clash with grounding-first architects (Tobias, Hana, Noor) over whether public pages can even confirm a signed-in identity surface, with the room settling on a provisional channel choice pending a live-product inventory. Across all views, the launch is gated on a shared artifact pack — job description, tone/voice guide, escalation matrix, versioned config with rollback, source registry with citations, and a scored eval plan with trace review.

    Key themes
    1. 01Channel choice and artifact set are one decision, not two: the launch channel must be the one where the artifacts are actually testable against logged transcripts
    2. 02Identity, consent, and traceability outrank ease-of-shipping and ambition when scoring candidate channels
    3. 03Grounding discipline: v1 answers only from a registered, cited public corpus with hard deferral on pricing, account state, and commitments
    4. 04Reversibility and observability: versioned config, pinned model, rollback drill, and scored eval with sampled trace review before go-live
    Blind spots
    1. !Public pages cannot confirm whether Keiki has a signed-in surface, verified identity, consent coverage, or real support volume — every ranking is inference until the live product is inventoried
    2. !Risk of treating the marketing site as the authoritative corpus when real answers may live in app APIs, order systems, or internal docs
    3. !Over-weighting volume in readiness scores can push the team toward a channel with thin identity and consent; over-weighting 'highest-scriptability' can do the same
    4. !Transcript-review and eval gates are only real if scored with pass/fail rubrics — otherwise they become ceremonial

          How to read it

          Public research on Keiki (onkeiki.com) suggests a consumer parenting/child-development content and product business (articles, guides, and app-style offerings aimed at parents of young children). For a customer-facing agent, the highest-value first channels are the ones where parents already ask Keiki questions and where answers are low-risk and reusable: the website/help center (FAQ and article Q&A), email/contact-form support, and in-product/app onboarding help. Social DMs and live chat should follow once answer quality is proven. The team needs a grounded knowledge base, an answer-style guide, escalation rules, a channel integration spec, and an evaluation set before launch.           13 30 / 60 / 90 rollout and evals · Target = a target, not a result

          30/60/90: what ships when, and how it's tested.

          Days0–30

          Stand up registry, crawl/index pipeline, and config repo; write the five launch-blocking artifacts (scope charter with handoff, registry and citation contract, versioned config with rollback, eval plan with thresholds, role/tone/escalation matrix); build golden set; internal dogfood on the owned web surface.

          Days60

          Limited public exposure on the single owned surface, public-content only, with feedback control and weekly trace review; go-live gated on offline pass criteria; first QHX deltas expected.

          Days90

          Full v1 on the owned surface; decide next channel (email, in-app, social DMs) from conversation logs; knowledge maintenance and continuous evaluation loops running with human sign-off; targets reviewed against online data and reset for v2.

          Offline evals

          (pre-release, every prompt/model/content change)

          • Golden set seeded from public pages plus labeled paraphrases, including adversarial out-of-scope questions.
          • Pass criteria Targets groundedness ≥ 95% of answers fully cited; refusal correctness ≥ 90% on out-of-scope set; boundary adherence 100% (zero transactional answers); correct escalation ≥ 95%; tone rubric pass ≥ 90%; regression suite zero regressions vs prior version.
          • Sampled trace review: human review of a fixed sample per release against the rubric; release blocked on any boundary breach.
          Online evals

          (production)

          • Feedback vote ratio and source-confirmation rate tracked per intent (targets set at launch, not asserted here).
          • Weekly sampled trace review; every gate failure triaged into the anti-pattern list.
          • Drift check: citation-gate failure rate and handoff rate monitored; threshold breach opens a ticket.
